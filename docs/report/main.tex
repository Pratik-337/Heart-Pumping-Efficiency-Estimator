\section{Introduction}
Left-ventricular ejection fraction (EF), the share of the end-diastolic volume that the left
ventricle ejects in one beat, is the most used single number of cardiac function: it defines
the reduced, mildly reduced and preserved classes of heart failure~\cite{bozkurt2021universal}.
Measuring it by hand from echocardiography videos is slow and varies between readers; video
networks now estimate it directly from apical four-chamber videos with errors close to that
variation~\cite{ouyang2020nature}.

Such models read short clips of a video, and the clip length sets both what the model can see
of the heartbeat and what it costs to run. EchoNet-Dynamic tuned this choice by sweeping the clip
length and sampling period, but reported only validation loss, for single runs, without
runtime or memory~\cite{ouyang2020nature}. This paper asks the question again with test-set
metrics, repeated seeds, measured cost and explanations that are scored rather than looked at.

We train R(2+1)D-18~\cite{tran2018closer} with 8 to 64 frames per clip under two sampling
regimes that separate how densely a clip samples the beat from how much of the beat it covers.
Our contributions are:
\begin{itemize}
\item a controlled frame-efficiency study on EchoNet-Dynamic with bootstrap confidence intervals
and Holm-corrected paired tests, and a zero-training baseline that feeds the released 32-frame
model fewer or more frames;
\item an accuracy--cost Pareto analysis with measured FLOPs, latency and memory on an 8\,GB
consumer GPU, for single-clip and multi-clip inference;
\item four numeric tests of frame and pixel attributions---alignment with the traced
end-diastolic and end-systolic frames, concentration inside the traced ventricle, a deletion
test and a weight-randomisation sanity check---and how they change with clip length.
\end{itemize}

\section{Related Work}
EchoNet-Dynamic~\cite{ouyang2020nature,ouyang2019ml4h} released \edaNAll{} annotated videos and an
R(2+1)D model that, combined with segmentation-guided beat-by-beat averaging, reached a test MAE
of 4.1 EF points; its Extended Data sweep of clip length (1--96 frames) and sampling period
(1--8) is the starting point of this study, which adds test metrics, seeds and cost.
Transformer models such as EchoCoTr~\cite{echocotr2022} report a test MAE of 3.95, a small gain
for more memory and compute. Foundation models trained on millions of videos, EchoPrime and
PanEcho~\cite{echoprime,panecho}, target whole multi-view studies rather than one view.
EchoGNN~\cite{mokhtari2022echognn} is explainable by design, predicting EF through learned
frame and video graphs. For post-hoc explanation we use Integrated
Gradients~\cite{sundararajan2017axiomatic} and Grad-CAM~\cite{selvaraju2017gradcam}, and follow
Adebayo et~al.~\cite{adebayo2018sanity} in checking that the maps depend on the trained weights.

\section{Dataset}
EchoNet-Dynamic holds \edaNAll{} apical four-chamber videos of 112$\times$112 pixels,
one per patient, with EF, end-diastolic and end-systolic volumes and expert tracings of the
left ventricle at end-diastole (ED) and end-systole (ES)~\cite{ouyang2019ml4h}. We use the
official split of \edaNTrain{} training, \edaNVal{} validation and \edaNTest{} test videos.
Videos have \edaFramesminAll{} to \edaFramesmaxAll{} frames (median \edaFramesmedianAll{}) at
a median of \edaFpsmedianAll{} frames per second; EF has mean \edaEfmeanAll{}\% (SD \edaEfsdAll{}), with
\edaNefleFourZeroAll{} videos at EF $\le 40$\%, \edaNefFourOneFourNineAll{} at 41--49\% and
\edaNefgeFiveZeroAll{} at $\ge 50$\%.

Each video is decoded once and cached as memory-mapped 8-bit frames. The red, green and blue
channels are not identical, so frames are converted with the standard luma weights. ED and ES are
assigned by the area of the traced ventricle (the larger is ED) rather than by file order;
\edaNhastraceAll{} videos have one regular tracing at each frame and so a defined ED and ES,
which the explanation tests need. A few videos have no tracings; as in EchoNet's own loader they
are left out of training and evaluation. Pixel mean and standard deviation for normalisation come
from the training videos only. Videos shorter than a clip's window are padded with mean-grey
frames, as EchoNet does; this affects \edaSharepaddedFsSixtyFour\% of videos at the longest
window (128 frames) and at most \edaSharepaddedFsThirtyTwo\% at the others.

\section{Method}
\subsection{Model}
All models are torchvision's R(2+1)D-18~\cite{tran2018closer,torchvision_r2plus1d} initialised
from Kinetics-400 weights, with the 400-class layer replaced by one linear output whose bias
starts at the mean training EF. Grey frames are copied to the three input channels. The network
pools over time and space, so it accepts any clip length.

\subsection{Sampling regimes}
A clip takes $T$ frames, one every $p$ raw frames, and so spans a window of $W = T p$ raw
frames. The \emph{fixed-stride} regime (FS) keeps $p = 2$, so the window grows with $T$; the
\emph{fixed-window} regime (FW) keeps $W = 64$ frames (about 1.28\,s, EchoNet's tuned setting)
and samples it more or less densely (Table~\ref{tab:configs}). FS-32 belongs to both regimes. A
window of $w$ seconds holds a whole beat only when the heart rate exceeds $60/w$ beats per
minute: FS-8 (0.32\,s) almost never sees a full beat, whereas every FW clip sees about one. A
drop in accuracy for FS-8 may therefore come from missing part of the beat rather than from
having few frames; FW-8 tells the two apart.

\subsection{Training}
Every configuration uses the same recipe: mean squared error, AdamW (learning rate
$10^{-4}$, weight decay 0.01), one warm-up epoch then cosine decay over 10 epochs, an
effective batch of 16 (micro-batch 8, two accumulation steps, so BatchNorm sees the same batch
size for every $T$), bf16 mixed precision with the output layer in FP32, and EchoNet's
augmentation: a random clip start and a random 112-pixel crop of the frame zero-padded by 12
pixels~\cite{ouyang2020nature}. Each epoch draws one clip per training video. The epoch with the
lowest validation MAE is kept. At $T = 64$, micro-batch 8 fits in 8\,GB only with activation
checkpointing, applied per residual block; it changes memory, not the computation. Each
configuration is trained with two seeds.

\subsection{Inference modes}
In \emph{M1} the model sees one clip centred in the video. In \emph{M2} clips start every $W/2$
frames and their predictions are averaged, as in common multi-clip testing; the number of clips,
and so the cost per video, depends on the window. M1 is the primary mode because it compares the
configurations' per-clip ability without the confound of different clip counts.

\subsection{Tier 0: frames without retraining}
As a training-free baseline we evaluate EchoNet's released 32-frame model~\cite{ouyang2020nature}
at all seven $(T, p)$ settings. It answers a deployment question---can a trained model simply be
fed fewer frames?---and gives a complete frame curve independent of our training.

\subsection{Explainability}
For 100 test videos (35, 15 and 50 per EF class, drawn once with a fixed seed) each model sees a
clip placed so that it contains both ED and ES; videos whose ED--ES gap exceeds the window are
counted and skipped. \emph{Temporal occlusion} replaces one frame at a time by the interpolation
of its neighbours and records the change in EF. \emph{Integrated
Gradients}~\cite{sundararajan2017axiomatic} attributes the output to every pixel, from a mean-grey
baseline (and a blurred-video baseline as a check). \emph{3D Grad-CAM}~\cite{selvaraju2017gradcam}
on the last convolutional stage gives coarse spatial maps; it has only $T/8$ time steps and is
not used for frames. Four tests score the maps: (i) the share of the two most important frames
within one sampled frame of ED or ES, against a permutation-test chance level; (ii) the share
of attribution inside the traced ventricle divided by the ventricle's share of the frame;
(iii) the mean change in EF when deleting frames from most to least important, against random
orders; (iv) the rank correlation between maps of the trained model and of models whose weights
are re-initialised from the output back to the input~\cite{adebayo2018sanity}. Attributions show
what moves the model's output, not what causes the heart's function.

\section{Experimental Setup}
Training, inference and all cost measurements ran on an NVIDIA RTX 4060 Laptop GPU (8\,GB) under
WSL2 with PyTorch 2.15, except that FW-8, FS-16 and FW-16 were trained on an RTX 4050 Laptop
GPU (6\,GB) with identical code, and an FS-8 run repeated
there measures the effect of the hardware. Model selection used validation data only; the test
set was evaluated once, after all models were frozen.

\emph{Metrics.} The primary endpoint is test MAE in M1. We also report RMSE, $R^2$,
Bland--Altman bias and 95\% limits of agreement~\cite{bland1986statistical}, the area under the
ROC curve for detecting EF $< 50$\% and EF $\le 40$\%~\cite{bozkurt2021universal}, MAE per EF
class, and the calibration slope of reference on predicted EF.

\emph{Statistics.} Confidence intervals are 95\% percentile bootstrap intervals over test videos
(10{,}000 resamples). Nine pre-specified comparisons---neighbouring $T$ within each regime, and
FS against FW at $T = 8, 16, 64$---use paired Wilcoxon signed-rank tests on per-video absolute
errors averaged over seeds, with Holm correction~\cite{holm1979simple} at $\alpha = 0.05$; the
effect size is the median paired difference with its bootstrap interval.

\emph{Cost.} FLOPs per clip are counted with PyTorch's FLOP counter (one multiply-add = two
FLOPs) and multiplied by the M2 clip count for the cost per video. Latency is the median of 50
timed runs after 10 warm-up runs, for one video at a time in bf16, timed with CUDA events. Peak
memory is measured for inference and for one training step; training time comes from the logs.
